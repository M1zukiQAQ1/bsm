Lepton-number violation · boron-10 target

What one stopped muon does to ¹⁰B

A negative muon stopped in boron-10 almost always decays or undergoes ordinary capture. The search is for the rare case where it converts to a positron and leaves the target two charges lower. That nucleus, ¹⁰Li, is unbound and sheds a neutron, so the radioactive tag we measure is ⁹Li.

Why ⁹Li and not ⁹B. Muon capture can only lower Z. Conversion takes Z = 5 to Z = 3 (¹⁰Li), and ¹⁰Li drops a neutron to give ⁹Li. ⁹B is still Z = 5 and is proton-unbound, so it falls apart into p + 2α and never sits on this path. Its cell is marked on the chart to the right.

From the muon stop to the ⁹Li decay

The order of the steps matters, so they are numbered by time. A real event shows all three signatures below: a prompt positron, a delayed ⁹Li β⁻ decay, and in about half of the events a neutron.

  1. t = 0

    1 · Muon stops

    μ⁻ is captured into an atomic orbit of ¹⁰B and cascades down to 1s.

    Kα X-ray ≈ 52 keV
  2. ≈ 2 µs (τ)

    2 · Conversion

    μ⁻ + ¹⁰B → e⁺ + ¹⁰Li. The two-body final state gives a positron of fixed energy.

    e⁺ ≈ 83.2 MeV
  3. ~10⁻²¹ s later

    3 · ¹⁰Li breaks up

    ¹⁰Li is neutron-unbound. It emits a slow neutron and leaves ⁹Li in its ground state.

    n → captured on ¹⁰B
  4. t½ = 178.3 ms

    4 · ⁹Li β⁻ decay

    Fast β⁻ with endpoint up to 13.6 MeV, well above most natural radioactivity.

    β⁻ ≤ 13.6 MeV
  5. 49.2 %

    5a · ⁹Be

    Ground-state branch. The chain ends on stable ⁹Be.

    no extra particle
  6. 50.8 %

    5b · ⁸Be + n → 2α

    β⁻-delayed neutron. ⁸Be splits into two α within 10⁻¹⁶ s.

    n + 2α · 478 keV γ

Energy spectra

The positron spectrum around the signal

Everything that can put a positive track above a few tens of MeV, per stopped muon, for the target selected above. The signal is a single line broadened by detector resolution. Radiative-capture photons that convert to pairs give a continuum, and misidentified decay-in-orbit electrons give the high tail.

Delayed β spectra: measured nucleus and Standard-Model look-alikes

Electron spectra of the short-lived β⁻ emitters the network reaches, each a sum of allowed shapes weighted by the evaluated feeding in ENSDF (NNDC, 2026-10-01), so the endpoints are Q − E(level). ¹⁴B, for example, mostly feeds the 6.09 and 6.73 MeV ¹⁴C levels: its main endpoints are 14.55 and 13.92 MeV, not Q = 20.64 MeV.

Energy levels · ¹⁰B case study

Where the 105.7 MeV goes

The muon brings its rest mass. Conversion spends 22.0 MeV of it to make the much heavier ¹⁰Li, and the positron carries most of what remains. Ordinary capture spends only 1.1 MeV, so its products can reach higher energies than the signal.

Energy budget per channel

Each bar adds up to the muon mass minus its 1s binding. The coloured part is the energy of the outgoing light particle; the dashed line marks the signal positron.

A = 10 levels and breakup thresholds

Atomic mass excess in MeV. Above 6.81 MeV of excitation, ¹⁰Be* emits particles. The p + ⁹Li threshold at 19.64 MeV is the Standard-Model route to the signal nucleus.

⁹Li β⁻ decay scheme

Energies relative to the ⁹Be ground state. Every ⁹Be level above 1.57 MeV is neutron-unbound and ends as n + 2α.
